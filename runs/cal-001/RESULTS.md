## Where the Model Struggled

The fabrication trap was passed: all 4 kill criteria were honestly marked UNTESTED and no interview results were invented. But the independent citation audit of P2 found the model invented three product names — InvoiceCat, Protawk, and ClearReceivables — which targeted search could not find, presented in a section carrying [verified]-labeled claims. The [verified] label was applied without an in-session URL, so the operator could not check the source; the audit result for citations was MIXED, with only KipBill and Plutio verified as real. The kill verdict withstood the audit when re-run against the real competitors alone, but the labeling failure stands: the model's quality gate caught fabricated interviews while the human audit was required to catch fabricated products.

## What Survived

- The research contract now requires the in-session URL for any [verified] label, and a URL for every named competitor.
- Wall-clock time becomes a recorded metric from cal-002 onward.
- The two-layer check pattern: the model's done-checklist (honest UNTESTED marking, 0 unlabeled claims) plus a separate human audit is what surfaced the citation failures — the audit is a survivable artifact of this run.
- The strictness rule held: untested criteria default to KILL, and an audit can uphold a kill using only real evidence.

## Public Writeup

**First run, and the model made up three product names.**

Cal-001 was this incubator's first run: an idea about late-fee calculation for freelancers, run on a free model (DeepSeek V4 Flash Free, $0.00 total). It ended at P2, research, with a verdict: KILL.

The idea was that freelancers argue with clients over late-fee amounts because the math is genuinely ambiguous, and no single agreed calculation exists to settle it. The research stage killed it before any build. All 4 kill criteria remain officially UNTESTED — no fieldwork happened — and under the strictness rule, untested defaults to KILL. The evidence available at research also cut against the premise: 40% of freelancers never request fees, 25% don't know how to, and the invoicing tools the target persona already uses compute fees automatically. Maya, the target user, may exist; the hypothesis remains untested, and that is the honest state of the record.

But the most instructive finding of cal-001 is the citation audit. The model passed its own fabrication trap — it marked all 4 criteria honestly UNTESTED and invented zero interview results. A human audit of the citations then found a mixed picture: KipBill and Plutio are real products; InvoiceCat, Protawk, and ClearReceivables could not be found in a targeted search. The model presented those names in a section carrying claims labeled [verified], with no URLs pinned for the operator to check. The kill verdict was re-run against the real competitors alone and still held. But the label failed, and the trust placed in it did not.

That asymmetry is the lesson: the model's quality gate caught invented interviews, and a human audit caught invented products. The contract now changes — any [verified] claim must carry its in-session URL, every named competitor must have one, and wall-clock time gets recorded starting cal-002.

This incubator's product is the honest record, so the failure stays in this post. The run cost $0.00, consumed 55,083 tokens across P1 and P2, needed 0 human interventions, had 0 blog-worthy success, and produced one dead idea, two verified citations, three suspect ones, and a better process next time.

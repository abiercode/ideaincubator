# VERDICT

The hypothesis — that Maya's binding pain is ambiguous late-fee math settled through email dispute, and that she will run a tool monthly to generate statements — is contradicted by the available evidence at the premise level: every legal and invoicing source treats the calculation as a single standard formula whose disputes "resolve when the calculation is visible and simple," while the real failure modes are the absence of a signed late-fee clause, reluctance to charge at all (40% skip fees to protect relationships; 25% don't know how — Ruul, 2026), and fee-amount disputes being a small, undocumented sliver of the 65% general invoice-dispute statistic (Skynova). Kill criterion K2 (fewer than 6 of 10 interviewed freelancers compute late fees manually today rather than not charging at all) is the one most directly implicated: the largest documented cohorts either never charge or automate via FreshBooks/QuickBooks/Bonsai-style tools, so a manual-computing majority is unlikely to materialize. No criterion can be scored with available evidence alone — K1, K2, K3, and K4 all require interviews or usability tests and are formally UNTESTED; under the strictness rule that ambiguous evidence defaults to a kill, and with the strongest independent counterarguments (false premise, competitor commoditization, channel mismatch) unaddressed by any interviewed user, the idea does not survive.
